RED · Immediate

Life-Threatening
Treat within minutes:
  • Cardiac arrest / no pulse
  • Severe respiratory distress (RR < 8 or > 30)
  • Major hemorrhage / shock (SBP < 90)
  • GCS ≤ 8 / unresponsive
  • Active seizure
  • Severe burns > 20% BSA
  • Anaphylaxis with airway compromise
  • Heat stroke (T > 40°C + AMS)
Target: ALS unit · transport priority 1

YELLOW · Urgent

Serious, Stable
Treat within 30-60 min:
  • Moderate respiratory distress (RR 25-30)
  • Stable chest pain
  • Closed fractures, dislocations
  • Burns 10-20% BSA
  • GCS 9-13
  • Heat exhaustion
  • Moderate dehydration
  • Stable trauma
Target: ALS or BLS · priority 2

GREEN · Walking Wounded

Minor
Treat when resources allow:
  • Minor cuts, abrasions
  • Sprains, contusions
  • Mild dehydration
  • Mild heat stress (cool, oral fluids)
  • Stable, ambulatory patients
  • Anxiety / panic without medical cause
Target: walk-in clinic · priority 3

BLACK · Expectant / Deceased

Not Survivable
Confirm and document:
  • Obvious mortal wounds (decapitation, etc.)
  • Cardiac arrest with no shockable rhythm + ROSC unlikely in mass casualty
  • Resources unavailable for survival
Target: respectful handling · time of death documented · MDL notify

Cardiac Arrest RED

  1. Confirm — check pulse 5-10 sec
  2. Start CPR — 100-120/min, depth 5-6cm
  3. Attach AED / monitor — analyze rhythm
  4. Shockable (VF/VT) → defibrillate, resume CPR 2 min
  5. IV/IO access · Adrenaline 1mg q3-5min
  6. Consider amiodarone 300mg after 3rd shock
  7. Treat reversible causes (4H 4T)
  8. Transport with mechanical CPR if available

Heat Stroke RED · Hajj

  1. T > 40°C + altered mental status = heat stroke
  2. Aggressive cooling immediately — ice packs (groin/axillae/neck), cool mist + fan
  3. Target: cool to 39°C in < 30 min
  4. IV fluids — NS 500mL bolus, repeat as needed
  5. Monitor for seizures, rhabdomyolysis
  6. Avoid antipyretics (ineffective)
  7. Transport rapidly — high mortality

Anaphylaxis RED

  1. IM Adrenaline 0.5mg (0.5mL of 1:1000) mid-thigh
  2. Repeat q5-15min if no response
  3. Lay flat (or sitting if dyspnea)
  4. O2 high-flow
  5. IV access — NS bolus if hypotensive
  6. Adjuncts: hydrocortisone 200mg IV, chlorphenamine 10mg IV
  7. Salbutamol neb if wheezing

Stable Chest Pain YELLOW

  1. 12-lead ECG ASAP — STEMI rules out
  2. Aspirin 300mg PO chew
  3. GTN 0.4mg SL if SBP > 90
  4. O2 if SpO2 < 94%
  5. IV access
  6. Morphine 2-5mg IV titrated for ongoing pain
  7. Transport to PCI-capable center

Active Seizure RED

  1. Protect airway — recovery position when convulsion ends
  2. O2 high-flow
  3. If > 5 min: Midazolam 10mg IM/buccal (5mg if < 40kg)
  4. IV access when safe
  5. Consider hypoglycemia → check BGL
  6. If pregnant + ≥ 20wks → eclampsia: MgSO4 4g IV/IM

Severe Hemorrhage RED

  1. Direct pressure with gauze
  2. Tourniquet for limb if uncontrolled
  3. Hemostatic dressing for junctional
  4. 2 large-bore IV — NS bolus
  5. TXA 1g IV within 3hr if available
  6. Permissive hypotension SBP target 90 (not in head trauma)
  7. Rapid transport, surgeon notification

Hypoglycemia YELLOW

  1. BGL < 4.0 mmol/L = hypoglycemia
  2. Conscious + can swallow → glucose tablets / juice
  3. Unconscious / NPO → Dextrose 10% 250mL IV over 5min
  4. Or Glucagon 1mg IM if no IV access
  5. Recheck BGL in 15 min
  6. Feed once awake — long-acting carbs
  7. Transport if persistent or insulin-related

Stroke (FAST) RED

  1. Face droop · Arm drift · Speech · Time
  2. Document onset time precisely
  3. BGL — rule out hypoglycemia
  4. O2 if SpO2 < 94%
  5. NPO — keep head 30° elevated
  6. Pre-alert stroke center
  7. Window: 4.5hr thrombolysis · 6hr thrombectomy

Dehydration / Heat Exhaustion YELLOW · Hajj

  1. Move to cool area — remove excess clothing
  2. Mild: ORS, cool fluids PO
  3. Moderate: IV NS 500mL bolus, monitor
  4. Severe: 2L NS bolus, electrolytes
  5. Watch for hyponatremia (over-hydration with water)
  6. Distinguish from heat stroke (no AMS, T < 40°C)

GCS Quick Reference

Eye Opening (E)

  • 4 — Spontaneous
  • 3 — To verbal command
  • 2 — To pain
  • 1 — None

Verbal Response (V)

  • 5 — Oriented
  • 4 — Confused conversation
  • 3 — Inappropriate words
  • 2 — Incomprehensible sounds
  • 1 — None

Motor Response (M)

  • 6 — Obeys commands
  • 5 — Localizes pain
  • 4 — Withdraws from pain
  • 3 — Abnormal flexion
  • 2 — Extension to pain
  • 1 — None
Total: 3-15. ≤ 8 = severe (intubation candidate). 9-12 = moderate. 13-15 = mild.

Stampede / Crush Injury

  • Scene safety FIRST — wait for crowd control
  • Triage RED for: chest crush, multiple fractures, AMS, hypotension
  • Crush syndrome: hyperkalemia, rhabdo
  • IV NS 1L bolus before extraction if entrapped > 30 min
  • Bicarbonate 50mmol IV if confirmed acidosis
  • Monitor cardiac for hyperkalemia (peaked T waves)

Heat Illness Spectrum

  • Heat cramps: rest, ORS, electrolytes
  • Heat syncope: lay flat, cool, fluids
  • Heat exhaustion: T < 40°C, +/- AMS, IV NS
  • Heat stroke: T > 40°C + AMS — RED, aggressive cooling
  • Pre-shifts: hydrate, ORS, breaks every 30 min

Pilgrim Medical Special Considerations

  • Many elderly with chronic illness (cardiac, diabetic, CKD)
  • Language barrier — use translators / picture cards
  • Modesty — gender-appropriate care where possible
  • Document Mahram (chaperone) for unaccompanied women
  • Common: foot blisters, sunburn, dehydration, fall
  • Religious sensitivity — explain procedures clearly

Mass Casualty (MCI) Activation

  • Trigger: ≥ 5 simultaneous serious casualties at one location
  • Notify OCC immediately — request MCI declaration
  • START triage (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)
  • Set up casualty collection point safely upstream
  • Roles: Triage / Treatment / Transport officers
  • Use color tags: red/yellow/green/black
  • Reds first to definitive care

Ihram-Specific

  • Pilgrims in ihram cannot wear sewn clothing — work around for IV access
  • Hair-cutting prohibited — shaving for surgical access only with permission
  • If pilgrim becomes unable to complete rituals, document for religious authority

Common Drug Interactions in Pilgrims

  • Anticoagulants — many on warfarin/DOACs
  • Beta-blockers — masks tachycardic response
  • Diabetic medications — fasting risks hypoglycemia
  • Diuretics — heat illness risk
  • Always check medication list before administration
